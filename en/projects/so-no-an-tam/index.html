01 · PRODUCT IDEA

Bring the things you keep remembering into one place you can see

When loans, due dates and past payments are scattered across messages, notes or memory, people have to keep reminding themselves. An Tâm creates a private space to record creditors, balances, payment schedules and transactions, then presents them in a way that can be checked and updated.

02 · PRODUCT DESIGN

A green identity that feels steady without feeling judgmental

The design prioritizes calm, legibility and privacy. Green guides attention toward states the user can act on; warning colors appear only when they add useful context. Balances are grouped by debt, due date and progress so people do not have to rebuild their whole financial picture in their head.

Logo and brand promise

The ledger suggests an orderly place to write things down; the shield and lock suggest protection; the bars and rising arrow show progress; the leaf suggests that lasting change takes time. The bound pages make an abstract finance task feel like a familiar personal tool.

Growth symbol

The chart and arrow do not promise to make debt disappear. They emphasize what a person can control: record what is known, choose a payment date and follow their own progress.

Private-vault symbol

The lock makes privacy visible, while the young plant frames progress as patient growth. The visual aims for safety without romanticizing debt.

Typography and reading rhythm

The app uses system sans-serif fonts for readable amounts and status labels, avoiding external font downloads. The Portfolio pairs a system serif for editorial headings with a system sans-serif for body copy; both render Vietnamese reliably across common devices.

Motifs and hierarchy

Rounded cards, fine separators, whitespace, and lock, calendar and chart icons establish a steady rhythm. Green signals states that are under control; amber helps identify approaching dates; red is reserved for verified warnings so the whole screen does not feel like an alarm.

“Kết tài chính cá nhân” can be read as connecting separate personal-finance facts into one manageable picture. The product line “Know who you owe, repay one step at a time, and find more peace each day” turns that promise into a repeatable sequence: understand first, schedule next, then record each action.

03 · USEFUL FEATURES

From a debt list to a report you can review

01

One-screen overview

See recorded obligations, paid and remaining amounts, open debts and repayment progress. Open a debt to review its balance, due date, installment, recent activity and history.

02

Debt register by status

Group each creditor, record date, due date, status and balance. Filter all, open, unverified and early-attention items; a record date is not mistaken for a payment deadline.

03

An auditable ledger

Each transaction is a line in the history. Corrections use a reversing entry with a reason instead of silent deletion; balances from before a starting point stay separate instead of being arbitrarily assigned to creditors.

04

A calendar that labels estimates

The monthly view gathers upcoming installments; days 29–31 move to the final day in shorter months. Projected installments do not change the balance, roll over automatically or count as payments.

05

Reports with clear limits

Review trends by month, quarter or year, repayment progress and debt groups. The payoff estimate is simple and excludes future interest, penalties and schedule changes. Shared reports are snapshots and can be public or password-protected, given an expiry date or revoked.

06

Privacy in everyday use

Search debts, hide displayed amounts, lock the vault and use automatic locking after inactivity. Due-date reminders move tasks out of memory without requiring constant attention to the balance.

07

Encrypted personal vault

The app states that data is encrypted on-device with AES-256-GCM and that keys are derived with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256 at 310,000 iterations. Cloudflare stores ciphertext; passwords and keys are not sent to the server. The account and vault use separate password layers.

04 · VISIBLE PROGRESS

How do a progress bar and donut chart clarify the debt picture?

An Tâm uses a progress bar on the overview and a donut chart in reports to show two core proportions: the recorded obligation already paid and the amount still outstanding. They turn figures people would otherwise have to add and compare into a visual signal that is quick to read, then point back to the debt records for verification.

01 · PROGRESS BAR

Shows direction and the portion completed

The bar places the amount paid beside the recorded total obligation, making the handled portion and what remains visible at a glance. After a transaction, this visual state offers recognizable feedback and helps people follow their routine instead of holding one large debt figure in memory.

02 · DONUT CHART

Places each share within the whole

The ring divides the recorded obligation into paid and outstanding portions. A percentage in the center and a nearby text legend make the two shares easy to compare. This format works well in a summary report, where people can step back to see the whole before reviewing details.

03 · READ THEM TOGETHER

Use the overview to orient; use the register to act

A progress bar reads like a journey; a donut makes the part-to-whole relationship explicit. They complement each other when used in different contexts, but repeat information if shown side by side without a distinct purpose. To see which creditor, due date or balance needs attention, people still need the debt register and payment calendar.

Design value: visualization reduces the need to remember and calculate, shifting attention from a vague “pile of debt” to a ratio people can verify. Seeing a small change may reinforce a sense of agency, but it does not guarantee faster repayment.

Read the metric carefully: the percentage is based on recorded obligations (paid + remaining), not elapsed time, a credit score or a payoff-date forecast. Its reliability depends on complete, accurate records; it does not calculate future interest, penalties or changes to terms. Verify creditor statements and review individual debts before planning.

05 · PSYCHOLOGY MEETS PRACTICE

Reduce the mental load; make the first step clear

Debt can be hard to manage because of uncertainty, overload or avoidance—not simply a lack of willpower. Holding many figures in mind makes the next action harder to see; avoidance may bring short relief while leaving dates and balances unclear. The app lowers friction by gathering facts, clarifying actions and keeping a reviewable record.

FRICTION · UNCERTAINTY

“How much do I owe, and to whom?”

Mechanism: one record for each creditor reduces working-memory demands.

Practice: confirm balances and due dates; review weekly.

FRICTION · AVOIDANCE

“I’ll deal with it later; I fear the number.”

Mechanism: neutral language, hidden amounts and a lock reduce judgment and privacy concerns.

Practice: do one small task: verify a due date or record a payment.

FRICTION · MISSED DATES

“When is the next payment due?”

Mechanism: calendar reminders attach an intention to a date while keeping projected plans distinct from verified obligations.

Practice: confirm the date and installment with the creditor.

FRICTION · DISCOURAGEMENT

“I paid, but can’t see progress.”

Mechanism: a ledger and progress view make recorded effort visible, providing feedback that can support a routine.

Practice: log a payment after making it; review monthly.

Limits: this is a behavioral-design interpretation, not a promise to treat anxiety or eliminate debt. The app helps organize information; repayment still depends on cash flow and creditor terms.

The CFPB financial well-being framework highlights day-to-day financial control. Research on the cognitive burden of scarcity helps explain why organizing information may help, but it did not evaluate this app: PNAS study.

06 · A ROUTINE THAT CAN LAST

Know the debt · verify the date · record each step

  1. Gather the facts. List each creditor, balance, installment, due date and anything that still needs verification.
  2. Verify before labeling. Mark a payment overdue only when the due date and unpaid installment are confirmed; do not infer it from an entry date.
  3. Choose a sustainable amount. Protect essential expenses. A projected schedule is a planning aid, not a change to creditor terms.
  4. Record after payment. Save the date, debt, amount and note; reverse an incorrect entry with a reason.
  5. Review on a cadence. Use reports to inspect changes, update facts and adjust the plan when cash flow changes.

An Tâm is a personal information-organizing tool. It does not connect to a bank to make payments, replace a contract or creditor confirmation, or provide professional financial advice. Demo figures are sample data; user counts and financial outcomes have not been published.
